P06 - The (Non-)Deficiency and (Non-)Redundancy of Clitic Pronouns

Project description

With a focus on clitic doubling (CD), this subproject poses the fundamental question of the ontology of the domain between agreement , clitics, and weak and strong pronouns, as well as the underlying nature of the categories it contains. The strong hypothesis is that these all instantiate the same grammatical class of elements, but differ in the size of the underlying structure, as well as in the features that trigger the realization of varying degrees of strength. Hypothesizing that the answer to the latter question lies in Kiparsky’s 2008 D-hierarchy (Kallulli, 2016, 2018, 2019), and assuming universal realization of all items in the hierarchy, this subproject aims to model the strength of realization with respect to different types of D and R , including operations such as impoverishment or rules of exponentiation. In particular, the hypothesis is put forward that the feature responsible for all repeated realizations is [topic], but that it is weakened in the process of grammaticalization.

Ultimately, doubling is generalized and driven by mere syntactic locality. What begins as topic doubling in a specific position ends as agreement with the highest argument or as universal clitic doubling. Depending on various factors, primarily the degree of grammaticalization and syntactic position, languages exhibit doubling effects of varying strength, from different null elements through agreement to clitics. The main empirical questions addressed by this subproject concern binding, reconstruction effects, resumption, the person-case constraint, and (anti-)locality effects associated with clitic pronouns. The comparison of languages with and without clitics or CD regarding the underlying semantic features informs Q1 and Q3, the way in which CD influences binding relationships is relevant for Q2, and the analysis of LF and PF effects in connection with CD informs Q4.

Team

Dalina Kallulli

Principal Investigator
University of Vienna

Dalina received her PhD in Theoretical Linguistics from Durham University in 1999. After several successful Fellowships, she was appointed first Assistant and then Associate Professor at the University of Vienna. She has worked on Voice and, more generally, argument structure (including verbal diminutives), clitic constructions, resumption, relatives and other wh-constructions. Read more

Adina Camelia Bleotu

Postdoc researcher
University of Vienna

Adina earned her Ph.D. in Linguistics from Ca’ Foscari University of Venice (2015), with a dissertation on morphological approaches to denominal verbs, later published as a monograph at Brill. Her research bridges theoretical linguistics, experimental approaches, and language acquisition, focusing on how children acquire structures involving logical operators, recursion, agreement phenomena and strong and weak pronouns.
