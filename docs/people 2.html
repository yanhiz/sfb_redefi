P05 – The Role of Self in Pronominal Coreference

Univ.-Prof. PhD Boban Arsenijević
Principal InvestigatorUniversity of Graz

boban.arsenijevic[at]uni-graz.at Institutional profile

Boban Arsenijević is Professor of Slavic Linguistics at the University of Graz. He earned a BA in Serbo-Croatian Language and Literature from the University of Niš, an MA in Linguistics from the University of Belgrade, and a Research MA and PhD in Linguistics from Leiden University. His research examines the interfaces of syntax with semantics and phonology, with particular emphasis on Slavic languages. His main interests include verbal aspect and morphology, clausal embedding, and semantic ontology. Within the SFB, he investigates similarities between intensified and reflexive pronouns that may underlie their diachronic and typological connections. His comparative research covers Slavic languages, where intensifiers may receive reflexive interpretations despite being formally distinct; German, where reflexives can combine with intensifiers; and Malayalam and English, where reflexive and intensified pronouns are formally identical.

Assoz. Prof. Dr. Dalina Kallulli
Principal InvestigatorUniversity of Vienna

dalina.kallulli[at]univie.ac.at Institutional profile

Dalina Kallulli earned an MA in English from the University of Tirana in 1991, an MPhil in General Linguistics from the University of Trondheim in 1995, and a PhD in Theoretical Linguistics from Durham University in 1999. Following several research fellowships, she joined the University of Vienna as Assistant Professor and was subsequently promoted to Associate Professor. Her research focuses on voice and argument structure, including verbal diminutives, as well as clitic constructions, resumption, relative clauses, and other wh-constructions.

Adina Camelia Bleotu is an Assistant Professor at the University of Bucharest. She received her PhD in Linguistics from Ca’ Foscari University of Venice in 2015. Her dissertation on morphological approaches to denominal verbs was subsequently published as a monograph by Brill. She has held fellowships at the University of Massachusetts Amherst and ZAS Berlin, and her research has appeared in journals including Language Acquisition, Glossa, and the Journal of Memory and Language. Her work combines theoretical linguistics, experimental methods, and language acquisition, with particular attention to children’s acquisition of logical operators, recursion, agreement, and strong and weak pronouns. Her current research examines null objects, reflexivity, reflexive clitics, and the Person–Case Constraint.

P07 — Deficiency and Redundancy in Iberoromance Pronominal Systems

Associate Prof. Mag. Dr. Albert Wall
Principal InvestigatorUniversity of Vienna

albert.wall[at]univie.ac.at Institutional profile

Albert Wall received his master’s degree in Romance and East Slavic Philology from the University of Tübingen in 2008 and completed his doctorate in Comparative Romance Linguistics at the University of Zurich in 2015. He joined the University of Vienna in 2020 and was appointed Associate Professor of Romance Linguistics in 2025. His research focuses on argument structure and the syntax and semantics of nominals in Ibero-Romance varieties. He also investigates the syntax–phonology interface and linguistic variation through experimental and other complementary methods. This methodological diversity enables him to examine theoretically significant phenomena that are difficult to observe directly.

Her research examines how syntactic, semantic, and discourse-related factors shape direct-object realisation in Spanish, with particular emphasis on differential object marking, clitic doubling, and null objects. P08 – Fading Reference – The Pragmaticalization of Pronoun + Verb

Univ.-Prof. Dr. Eva-Maria Remberger
Principal InvestigatorUniversity of Vienna

eva-maria.remberger[at]univie.ac.at Institutional profile

Eva Maria Remberger studied Romance Languages and Literatures, German, and English at the University of Cologne, as well as German as a Foreign Language at the University of Bonn. She completed her doctorate at Freie Universität Berlin in 2003. From 2007 to 2008, she was a Feodor Lynen Fellow of the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation at the University of Cambridge and Clare Hall. She served as Junior Professor of Romance Linguistics at the University of Konstanz from 2006 to 2013 and has been Professor of Romance Linguistics at the University of Vienna since 2013. Her research spans grammatical theory, modality, volitionality, evidentiality, discourse-marker development, and the morphology and formal pragmatics of Romance languages from synchronic and diachronic perspectives. She also investigates language contact and connections among migrant languages, Romance languages, and Latin in language teaching and subject didactics.

P09 – Animacy Features and the Flexibility of Pronouns

Assoz. Prof. Mag. Dr. Steffen Heidinger
Principal InvestigatorUniversity of Graz

steffen.heidinger[at]uni-graz.at Institutional profile

Steffen Heidinger is Associate Professor of Romance Linguistics at the University of Graz. He completed his doctorate in 2008 within a Franco-German graduate programme involving the universities of Stuttgart and Paris 8. His dissertation examined the diachronic development of French anticausatives. In 2017, he completed his habilitation in Romance Linguistics at the University of Graz with a thesis on the information-structural properties of Spanish depictives. His current research focuses on the animacy features of French and Spanish pronouns. Within the subproject “Animacy Features and the Flexibility of Pronouns,” he investigates whether particular pronoun types are specified for animacy and how redundancy and deficiency license uses that diverge from these specifications, such as strong pronouns referring to inanimate entities.
Dr. Yanis da Cunha, Master
Postdoc researcherUniversity of Graz

yanis.da-cunha[at]uni-graz.at Institutional profile

Yanis da Cunha is a researcher in Romance Linguistics at the University of Graz. His doctoral research at Université Paris Cité examines gender effects on grammatical-function assignment, including biases that favour male subjects and female objects. More broadly, he investigates the factors underlying syntactic alternations, particularly variation in voice and word order across English, French, Modern Greek, and Spanish. Within the subproject “Animacy Features and the Flexibility of Pronouns,” he studies the role of gender in French and Spanish pronominal systems. His research explores how grammatical gender affects the animacy restrictions associated with certain pronouns, including French strong pronouns, and how pronouns accommodate mismatches between grammatical and semantic gender, such as reference to gender-underspecified nouns.
